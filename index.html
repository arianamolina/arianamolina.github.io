The Impact of Energy Renovation on Swiss Household Health

A Longitudinal Study from 2013–2023

Master's Thesis | University of Neuchâtel | 2025

Evolution of Cantonal Subsidies over Time (2013–2021)

This interactive map showcases the evolution of cantonal subsidies given by Le Programme Bâtiments over the years to different households for each canton. The data reveals significant regional variations in energy renovation support, with larger cantons like Bern, Zurich, and Vaud consistently providing higher subsidy amounts.

Policy Variation Across Cantons

Subsidy levels vary significantly across Swiss cantons and over time, creating exogenous variation used as an instrumental variable.

Why It Matters

This variation allows identification of the causal impact of renovations on health outcomes.

Key Insight

Higher subsidy intensity is associated with increased renovation activity, which drives the estimated health improvements.

Conclusion

Energy-efficient renovations have a positive but modest causal effect on health. Using an IV-Probit model, living in a renovated dwelling increases the probability of reporting "very good" health by about +1.9 percentage points, with a statistically significant effect (p ≈ 0.06) and a strong instrument (F = 17.25).

Overall, the results suggest that housing improvements contribute to better well-being, although the magnitude of the effect remains limited.

How to calculate the CO2 of a company?

A company's carbon footprint is calculated by measuring all greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions generated by its activities. These emissions are usually expressed in CO₂ equivalent (CO₂e). The international standard most companies follow is the GHG Protocol, which divides emissions into three categories called Scopes 1, 2, and 3.

What are Scopes 1, 2, and 3?

Scopes 1, 2, and 3 classify a company's greenhouse gas emissions according to the GHG Protocol.

Scope 1

Direct emissions from sources owned or controlled

Scope 2

Indirect emissions from purchased electricity and energy

Scope 3

all of the other indirect emissions

How to start calculating then?

First, we need to conduct preliminary research to categorize the different scopes using a visual delimitation chart. This requires understanding all company activities, the full A-to-Z lifecycle of the products, and the entire waste management process. You can start with a chart like this one:

How to retrieve the data?

Once the delimitation chart is completed, the next step is collecting data across all departments. This is often the most challenging phase, as emissions can either be estimated or calculated precisely in kilograms or a monetary unit for a specific item or activity. During this stage, a commuting questionnaire for employees is also essential to assess transportation-related emissions.

To convert the collected data into "CO₂ equivalent" (CO₂e), we use what are called emission factors, which act as conversion factors between an activity and its associated emissions. These emission factors can be obtained from databases such as Climatiq.io, ecoinvent, ADEME, or EXIOBASE.

What steps to take in the future in order to reduce the CO2 of a given company?

Once a company has calculated its CO₂ emissions for each department, the next step is to establish clear reduction targets using appropriate KPIs. This stage is essential, as it transforms carbon data into actionable strategies and measurable environmental impact.
